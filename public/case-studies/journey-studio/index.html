The setting

A car loan is a team sport.

Rupyy lends on used and new cars in India. Every loan passes through four teams, each with its own app. A journey is everything those teams see and do on one loan, from filing to the last document.

Terms you'll see Disbursal paying out the loan · RC the car's registration certificate · PDD post-disbursal documents, such as the new RC · PRD product requirements document

  1. SalesFiles the case with the customer and tracks it to the end. Phone.
  2. Doc CheckerChecks every document before money moves. Desktop.
  3. Payment BoxApproves and pays the fees around the loan. Desktop.
  4. RTO agentMoves the car's registration at the RTO, the regional transport office. Phone.
01The tension

A journey isn't a stack of screens.

After disbursal, three teams work the same loan at once, and each waits on another. Our Figma prototypes followed one person down one happy path, so these handovers, the hardest part to get right, were first tested when developers built them.

  • Copies driftEvery status needed its own screen. Agent Payments alone had 17 near-identical copies to keep in step.
  • Spec in piecesThe flow, the screens, the handovers and the status rules lived in Figma, Jira and a spreadsheet.

Our PMs prototyped in Claude Design, Anthropic's AI prototyping tool, before Journey Studio existed, and still do when an idea needs a quick first look. Each tool has its own job.

  • Claude Design One screen, fast, to see if an idea is worth more time.
  • Journey Studio The whole journey, every team, checked and handed to developers.
  • Figma Under both, as the design master the Design Kit is matched to.

03The insight

Make the flow the source of truth.

If the flow decides which screens exist, the prototype can't drift from it. Screens hang off shapes, shapes carry teams, and because the whole journey is one graph, the studio can walk it and check it.

01The flow decides the screens

No screen exists outside the flow, so the spec and the prototype are the same thing.

02Real screens, on brand

Screens are HTML built only from Design Kit parts and tokens, so nothing on a screen is invented.

03Fix once, fixed everywhere

Near-identical screens are states of one base; repeated pieces are shared parts.

04The studio checks itself

Every load re-walks the graph for dead ends, broken links, size and design issues.

Shared parts

Fix once, fixed everywhere

Pieces that repeat across screens live once as shared parts. Each screen keeps a placeholder with only what differs, so a change to the case header lands on all 73 screens that use it. Home lists every part, where it's used and how often. I first hid parts to keep Home light; PMs needed to see what a change would touch, so they came back. A part that's useful beyond one journey is proposed to the Design Kit and comes back to every studio in the next release.

States

Agent Payments · 1 base, 16 states

A screen that differs from another by a few words is stored as a state: the base screen plus a short list of text changes. Agent Payments went from about 880 KB of copies to a 54 KB base and about 125 KB of patches, and editing the base updates all 16 states.

Any product, any device

Sales, Ops and Partner live today

Nothing in the studio knows about loans. A project is any product, a journey any entry point, a team any role and a theme any brand at any size. The same tools run Sales, Ops and Partner. Partner runs as its own workspace only because it's a separate platform.

Looking back
What I learned
  1. The model matters more than the canvas.Once the flow was the source of truth, checks, numbering and Preview fell out of it.
  2. Dedupe early.I chose states over a screen per status. States and shared parts cut screen data by more than half and made fixes one-line changes.
  3. Show other teams, don't skip them.Seeing every team on one case made handovers a design topic, not a footnote.
What I'd do differently
  1. Model handovers on day one.The first versions drew screens first and handovers later. The gaps were always in the handovers.
  2. Put engineering in Preview earlier.The feasibility round got shorter every time developers saw the running journey sooner.
  3. Measure from the start.Review rounds and issues caught were counted after the fact. They belong in the studio.
What's next
  1. A second product.Open the studio to a team beyond lending.
  2. Sessions in review.Show test-session results next to the comments on each screen.
  3. Numbers in the studio.Count review rounds and issues caught per journey, every release.
