01 · Why I made it

I wanted it. It didn't exist.

I take photos of small things: a chai stall, a sign I like, the sea at the end of a walk. They sank into my gallery between screenshots. Every journal app I tried opened on a blank page and asked me to write first, and by the evening I’d forgotten why the photo mattered.

a · moment first

Catch it now, write later.

Journal apps open on a blank page and wait for you to have something to say. The moment is usually gone by then. I wanted the app to open on the camera, so keeping a moment takes one tap, and the words can come in the evening.

Snapchat had the camera I wanted: open it, shoot, done. But everything in it is made to be sent to someone. I wanted that speed with nobody on the other end.

b · mine

My diary isn't anyone's data.

No sign-up, no login, no server of mine to keep it on. Entries, photos and voice notes live on the phone. Backup is something you turn on, and it goes to your own Google Drive.

A diary is the most personal thing on my phone. I didn’t want an account or a company between me and it, and I didn’t want to run a server either.

c · feels like mine

A page I'd actually keep.

Drawing and writing on photos, stickers of real things, you and what you're looking at in one picture. Small things that make a page feel like a scrapbook, not a form.

A journal I’d keep looks more like a scrapbook than a form: a doodle on a photo, a ticket stuck on the page, my face in the corner of what I was looking at.

Where your journal lives

On the phone. That's the whole map.

There's no Keppo server in this picture, because there isn't one. The only two things that ever reach the internet are both off until you turn them on: a backup to your own Google Drive, and the map's tiles.

Underneath: one SQLite database and the photos and videos as files on the phone. The backup copies a snapshot of the database plus any media not already in your Drive, and never deletes anything there. The app's Drive permission only sees files it made.

first nameDear Diary

The working name. Too plain to find in an app store, and it sounded like a children’s diary.

thenNotica

An invented word, so it could be searched for and trademarked, with a bit of “notice” in it.

since 1 Oct 2026Keppo Journal

The second app in the Keppo family, after Keppo Moto. Keppo Moto already kept my rides; a journal that keeps my days belongs next to it.

02 · One moment, start to finish

Shoot first. It becomes a page later.

One moment, followed through the app. An evening chai at a stall on Lantern Street, just after the rain. (A demo moment, staged for this page.)

1 Open

It opens on the camera.

There's no home screen to get past. Open the app and you're looking through the camera, even with the phone locked: the Quick Settings tile opens it over the lock screen, and the rest of the journal stays locked.

Underneath: over the lock screen the app shows only its capture deck (the camera, a note and the unsaved tray). Anything already saved waits until you unlock.

2 Shoot

Tap for a photo. Hold for a video.

While holding, slide up to zoom; slide left to keep recording hands-free. One button in the top bar puts you and what you're looking at in the same photo.

Underneath: the journal's own camera, built on Android's CameraX. One camera does photos, video, both cameras at once and stickers, so switching between them doesn't go black.

3 The tray

Not filed yet, and that's fine.

Each capture drops into the thumbnail in the bottom-left corner. It waits there, unsaved, as long as you like: shoot five things and decide later what they belong to.

Underneath: the tray survives the app being closed. It's the only place the app holds things you haven't committed to.

4 Make it yours

Draw on it. Write on it.

Open the tray and every photo can take a doodle, a few words, a filter, a crop, or a sticker you made from something real.

Underneath: drawings and text stay editable on top of the photo, never burnt in, so you can change them a year later.

5 It's an entry

File it. Everything comes along.

Filing turns the tray into an entry: the photos, the video, a few lines you type. Everything comes along unless you take it out. Each piece keeps its own time and place, so a page written over a day still tells you where each bit happened.

Underneath: every block carries its own time and place. A small chip shows only where it changes, and blocks written far away get their own pin on the map.

Open · the tile, with the phone still locked
The bottom-left thumbnail always means: captured, not filed yet.

Most journals ask you to compose first. This one keeps first and composes later. The tray is what makes that safe: nothing you shoot is lost, and nothing is filed until you say so.

03 · The fun bits

Things I made because I wanted them.

None of these were on a roadmap. Each one started as "it would be nice if…". Four of them, and the reason each one exists.

Stickers

Stickers from real things.

Swipe up on the camera and it starts looking for shapes: a sign, a poster, a sticker on a laptop. Steady for a moment and the outline turns marigold; take it, and it's a sticker for your pages or a WhatsApp pack.

Signs and stickers I liked kept ending up as photos I never opened again. As stickers they go on my pages, and into WhatsApp.

Front + back

You and what you're looking at.

One press takes both cameras (on phones that can run both at once; others take one, then the other). Tap the little bubble to swap which one is big. Each photo keeps its own doodles and text, even after a swap.

A photo of a view never shows who was there. One press, and it’s both.

Lock screen

Capture without unlocking.

The moment doesn't wait for a fingerprint. Over the lock screen you get the camera, a quick note and the unsaved tray, with the same screens as usual. Everything you already kept stays behind the lock.

By the time I’d unlocked the phone, the moment had moved on.

Keppo Moto

Rides write themselves in.

Keppo Moto, my riding app, records the ride; the journal turns it into an entry with the route and the moments it filmed, by itself. Rename the ride in Moto and the entry follows, unless you renamed it yourself.

I record every ride already. Typing it up again in a journal was never going to happen.

Widgets

13 widgets, every size.

A quick note, today's question, on this day, a photo wall. Each one lays itself out for the size you give it.

The camera

A camera of its own.

Tap to focus, drag the sun for brighter or darker, Night and HDR where the phone offers them. Built in five steps, and the app got 6.1 MB smaller.

04 · Built with AI

A designer, an AI, and my phone.

I don't write code. I designed every screen, decided what to build and what to cut, and tested each build on my own phone. The AI wrote the code and explained each problem to me in plain words, so I could make the call.

What I did

  • Designed the screens and the flow
  • Decided what to build, and what to cut
  • Tested every build on my phone
  • Wrote the spec and the decisions log before any code, and kept them current

What the AI did

  • Wrote the app: TypeScript, plus Kotlin for the native parts
  • Wrote the 218 automated tests that keep it standing
  • Explained problems in plain language and gave options
  • Kept the project notes current, so each session started where the last ended

Hard problem · the camera

Every switch went black.

What broke
The app used three separate cameras: one for photos and video, one for front + back, one for stickers. Moving between them meant one had to let go before the next could start, so the screen went black for a moment, every time.
What we tried
Shorter hand-overs, a fade to hide the gap, and remembering phones that couldn't run both cameras at once. It got better, never good.
What shipped
The journal's own camera, built in five steps: photos and video, then front + back, then stickers, then focus and Night/HDR, then removing the old ones. One camera, no hand-over. The app got 6.1 MB smaller too.

Hard problem · the lock screen

"Open the camera" kept asking for my fingerprint.

What broke
I wanted a double-press of the power button to open the journal's camera, like a phone's own camera app. My phone's double-press always asks for an unlock before opening any app, and its "Camera" option only opens the phone maker's camera.
What we tried
A special front door for the app that Android allows over the lock screen, and offering the journal as a camera app. A background listener for the power button was possible, but it meant always running in the background, so I said no.
What shipped
The Quick Settings tile opens the camera over the lock screen, and the app icon works locked too. The journal shows only its capture deck there; everything already saved waits for the unlock.

The two I picked. Others, just as real: widgets showing old states over new ones, and a Drive restore that can never lose the journal.

Say what, not how.

I never told the AI how to build anything. I described what I wanted to happen on the screen, and what was wrong when it didn’t. That was enough.

The phone is the only real test.

Tests catch logic. Only my own phone caught a black camera preview, a drag that died after a centimetre, a widget showing yesterday.

Write the decisions down.

Every session starts from zero. A handoff note and a log of why each call was made meant nothing got decided twice.
